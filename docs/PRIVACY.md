# Privacy

## Plain-language summary

CrossDrop is designed around direct device-to-device local transfers. It does
not require an account, cloud storage, central transfer server, advertising
profile, or telemetry service for normal operation.

Files and messages move directly between the devices taking part in a transfer.

## What stays on your devices

CrossDrop stores the following locally to provide its features:

- device identity and trusted-device records;
- local labels, pairing state, and last-seen connection details;
- transfer and message history needed for the app's History and Activity views;
- received files in the chosen CrossDrop receipt location; and
- Android/Windows notification and preference settings.

## What is sent to another CrossDrop device

To discover and connect locally, participating devices exchange the limited
network and identity information needed to identify a CrossDrop service. During
a transfer, the recipient receives the file or text chosen by the sender and
the information necessary to show and approve it.

Pairing and transfer traffic is encrypted in transit. You should still verify
the pairing code when CrossDrop requests it.

## What CrossDrop does not do by design

- It does not upload your files or messages to a CrossDrop cloud service.
- It does not require a user account.
- It does not run advertising or analytics tracking for normal operation.
- It does not index all of your device files in the background.

## Your choices

You can reject incoming requests, remove a trusted device, clear local history
where available, turn off Android keep-ready mode, or uninstall the app.

Do not transfer files to a person or device you do not trust. CrossDrop cannot
recover a file after you deliberately send it to another device.
